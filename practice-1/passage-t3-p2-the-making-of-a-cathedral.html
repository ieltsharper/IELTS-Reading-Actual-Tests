The Making of a Cathedral

A A great medieval cathedral was the largest and most expensive object its region would ever produce, and almost nobody who began one lived to see it finished. Work went forward in campaigns rather than at a steady pace: a bishop raised money, a few bays went up, the money ran out, and the site stood quiet until a new benefactor, a fresh wave of gifts or the arrival of pilgrims at a shrine made it possible to start again. Decades, and sometimes centuries, separated the first stone from the last. Because of this, the east end of the church, containing the high altar, was generally completed first and walled off, so that services could be held there while the nave beyond was still a building site. It also explains why so many cathedrals change style as the eye travels westwards.

B The buildings that we call Gothic were made possible by three devices used together. The pointed arch carries its load more steeply downwards than a round one, and, more usefully still, arches of different widths can be made to rise to the same height, which allows a vault to be built over a bay that is not square. The rib vault concentrates the weight of a ceiling onto a framework of stone ribs, which deliver it to a few points rather than spreading it along the whole wall. The flying buttress then carries the outward thrust from those points across the open air to a heavy pier standing clear of the building. Once the thrust has somewhere else to go, the wall between the piers is no longer holding the roof up. It can be opened, and the result is the cathedral as a cage of stone filled with glass.

C Directing all this was the master mason, a craftsman who had worked his way up with a chisel and who combined the roles later divided between architect, engineer and site manager. He worked from a lodge, a timber building against the wall of the church where the masons cut stone, kept their tools and ate. Drawings on parchment were rare, small and costly, and the serious design work was done at full size. Many lodges had a tracing floor, a wide surface of soft plaster on which the outline of a window, an arch or a moulding could be set out at its true dimensions with a compass and a straight edge, scraped away and drawn again. From these full-size outlines the masons cut thin templates in wood, and every block was worked until it matched its template. The method was geometrical rather than arithmetical: proportions were generated by construction, by squares and circles drawn from one another, at a time when written calculation was laborious and few on site could do it.

D The material itself had to be found, shaped and moved. Where a good bed of stone lay near the site the builders quarried it there; otherwise it came from far off, and the first rule of transport was that stone should be roughly shaped at the quarry, so that less weight had to be carried. Wherever a river or the sea offered a route the stone went by water, since a single barge could take what would have needed dozens of carts over bad roads. At the site each block was finished by hand, and a mason would cut a small mark of his own into the stones he had dressed so that his output could be counted and paid for; these marks can still be read on the inner faces of many walls. Cathedrals also consumed timber on an enormous scale, and a large site kept its own smiths busy doing little but sharpening chisels.

E Raising the stone was a problem of its own. An arch cannot stand until its last stone is in place, so each one was built over a wooden framework, called centring, which held the blocks in position until the mortar had set and could then be struck away and used again. Scaffolding rose with the walls, its horizontal poles resting in square holes deliberately left in the masonry, some of which were never filled in. For lifting, the great machine was a crane driven by a large wooden wheel, inside which one or two men walked as though climbing an endless staircase, turning a drum that wound the rope. The crane was mounted on the walls themselves and moved upwards as they grew. To grip the block, masons cut a tapering slot in its upper face and fitted into it an iron device known as a lewis, whose wedge-shaped pieces were pulled tight by the weight of the stone, so that the heavier the load, the firmer the hold.

F The pace of the work was set, in the end, by mortar. Medieval mortar was made from lime, and lime mortar hardens slowly, drawing in air over months rather than curing in hours. A wall that rose too quickly could squeeze and spread the soft joints beneath it, and frost reaching mortar that had not yet set would destroy it altogether. Masons therefore worked through the warmer months only, and at the end of the season the tops of unfinished walls were capped with straw, dung or turf to keep the frost out until spring. The need to let one stage settle before loading it with the next is one reason why a cathedral could not have been built quickly even if the money had never run short.

G Not everything stood. The builders were working beyond any tested experience, and the limit was found at Beauvais in northern France, where the choir was raised higher than any before it and part of its vault came down a few years after completion. It was rebuilt with the supports doubled, and the church was never finished as planned. Elsewhere the warnings were quieter: cracks appeared, piers leaned, and later masons added flying buttresses to buildings that had been designed without them. What the sites did not use was the army of pious volunteers of popular imagination. Processions of townspeople dragging carts of stone did occur, and were remembered because they were unusual; the walls were built by paid men, by quarriers, masons, carpenters, smiths, glaziers and plumbers who moved from one great project to the next, and the chief difficulty for any bishop was less a shortage of faith than a shortage of cash.

Bước 3 – Đọc One-Shot: đến đoạn nào chốt hết câu hỏi của đoạn đó

Đoạn A – Nhà thờ lớn được xây theo từng đợt tùy tiền quyên góp, kéo dài hàng chục đến hàng trăm năm, nên phần đông (nơi có bàn thờ chính) được hoàn thành trước và ngăn lại để hành lễ.

  • Câu 14 → A. "a reason why one part of the church was normally completed before the rest" ↔ "east end ... generally completed first" + lý do "so that services could be held there". Trích: "the east end of the church, containing the high altar, was generally completed first and walled off, so that services could be held there while the nave beyond was still a building site"
  • Câu 23 → FALSE. Câu hỏi nói công việc "continued at a constant rate"; bài khẳng định ngược lại "in campaigns rather than at a steady pace" – tiền hết thì công trường nằm im. Mâu thuẫn trực tiếp nên FALSE, không phải NOT GIVEN. Trích: "Work went forward in campaigns rather than at a steady pace"
  • Lưu ý: Đoạn này giải thích luôn vì sao phong cách kiến trúc đổi dần khi đi về phía tây – chi tiết đẹp nhưng không có câu hỏi nào hỏi.

Đoạn B – Kiến trúc Gothic dựa trên ba sáng chế dùng chung: vòm nhọn, vòm có sườn và trụ bay, nhờ đó bức tường không còn phải đỡ mái và có thể mở ra thành kính.

  • Lưu ý: Nhiều từ kỹ thuật (pointed arch, rib vault, flying buttress) nhưng không có câu hỏi; đừng dừng lại tra nghĩa từng từ, chỉ cần nắm ý "tường không còn chịu lực nữa".

Đoạn C – Master mason là người chỉ huy, làm việc ở lodge; bản vẽ trên giấy da thì hiếm và đắt, nên thiết kế thật sự được vẽ ở kích thước thật trên sàn thạch cao rồi cắt thành dưỡng gỗ.

  • Câu 16 → C. "shapes being worked out at their true size" ↔ "the outline of a window, an arch or a moulding ... set out at its true dimensions". Trích: "a tracing floor, a wide surface of soft plaster on which the outline of a window, an arch or a moulding could be set out at its true dimensions"
  • Câu 26 → FALSE. Câu hỏi nói thiết kế "usually drawn out on parchment"; bài nói giấy da "rare" và thiết kế nghiêm túc làm ở cỡ thật. "Rare" phủ định "usually" nên FALSE. Trích: "Drawings on parchment were rare, small and costly, and the serious design work was done at full size"
  • Câu 24 → NOT GIVEN. Đoạn này mô tả vai trò và cách làm việc của master mason nhưng không nói gì về tiền công của ông so với thợ khác; đoạn G chỉ nói chung là "paid men". Bẫy: thấy "master" nên dễ suy diễn rằng ông lương cao nhất – đó là suy luận ngoài bài.
  • Lưu ý: Câu dài cuối đoạn có xương sống "The method was geometrical rather than arithmetical" (AI + LÀ GÌ), rồi dấu hai chấm giải thích "proportions were generated by construction", và mệnh đề "at a time when written calculation was laborious" nêu hoàn cảnh – tức là họ dùng hình học vì tính toán bằng chữ số quá khó.

Đoạn D – Đá phải được tìm, đẽo thô ngay tại mỏ cho nhẹ bớt rồi chở bằng đường thủy nếu có thể; ở công trường thợ hoàn thiện từng khối và khắc dấu riêng để được tính công.

  • Câu 19 → weight. Flow-chart lặp gần nguyên văn: "so that less ___ has to be carried" ↔ "so that less weight had to be carried". Trích: "stone should be roughly shaped at the quarry, so that less weight had to be carried"
  • Câu 20 → water. "travels by ___, because one barge can take the load of many carts" ↔ "went by water, since a single barge could take what would have needed dozens of carts". Bẫy: đừng viết "river" hay "barge" – chỗ trống đi sau "by" nên phải là "water". Trích: "Wherever a river or the sea offered a route the stone went by water, since a single barge could take what would have needed dozens of carts"
  • Câu 21 → mark. "cuts his own ___ into it, so that his work can be counted" ↔ "cut a small mark of his own ... so that his output could be counted". Trích: "a mason would cut a small mark of his own into the stones he had dressed so that his output could be counted and paid for"

Đoạn E – Việc nâng đá cần giàn gỗ (centring) đỡ vòm, giàn giáo gác vào lỗ chừa sẵn, cần cẩu quay bằng bánh xe gỗ có người đi bên trong, và kẹp lewis giữ khối đá càng nặng càng chặt.

  • Câu 15 → E. "how blocks were held securely while being raised" ↔ cơ chế của lewis: "To grip the block" và "the heavier the load, the firmer the hold". Trích: "masons cut a tapering slot in its upper face and fitted into it an iron device known as a lewis, whose wedge-shaped pieces were pulled tight by the weight of the stone, so that the heavier the load, the firmer the hold"
  • Câu 22 → wheel. "A crane driven by a wooden ___, in which men walk" ↔ "a crane driven by a large wooden wheel, inside which one or two men walked". Trích: "the great machine was a crane driven by a large wooden wheel, inside which one or two men walked as though climbing an endless staircase"
  • Lưu ý: Centring cũng là cách "giữ" đá, nhưng là giữ đá khi vòm đã lắp xong chứ không phải "while being raised"; chi tiết đúng cho Q15 là lewis.

Đoạn F – Tốc độ xây rốt cuộc do vữa vôi quyết định: vữa cứng rất chậm, xây nhanh quá thì mạch bị ép, và sương giá phá hỏng vữa chưa kịp đông, nên thợ chỉ làm vào mùa ấm và che đầu tường qua đông.

  • Câu 17 → F. "a weather condition that could ruin work already done" ↔ "frost ... would destroy it altogether"; frost = hiện tượng thời tiết. Trích: "frost reaching mortar that had not yet set would destroy it altogether"

Đoạn G – Không phải mọi công trình đều đứng vững: vòm ở Beauvais sập vì xây quá cao; và tường được dựng bởi thợ ăn lương chứ không phải đoàn tình nguyện viên mộ đạo như truyền thuyết.

  • Câu 18 → G. "an example of a building whose design went beyond what was safe" ↔ Beauvais xây cao hơn mọi công trình trước và vòm sập. Bẫy: đoàn dân thị trấn kéo xe đá có thật nhưng bài nói đó là chuyện hiếm ("remembered because they were unusual"), không làm câu 25 sai. Trích: "the walls were built by paid men, by quarriers, masons, carpenters, smiths, glaziers and plumbers"
