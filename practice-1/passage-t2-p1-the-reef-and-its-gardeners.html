The Reef and Its Gardeners

A A coral reef looks like rock, and in a sense it is, but it is rock made by animals. The builder is the coral polyp, a small, soft-bodied creature that is little more than a column of tissue crowned with a ring of stinging tentacles. Polyps are relatives of the sea anemones and the jellyfish, and like them they catch food by stinging it. What makes the reef-building corals different is a chemical habit: they take calcium and carbonate from the surrounding seawater and lay them down as limestone underneath themselves, so that each animal sits in a small stony cup of its own making. A polyp divides to produce another polyp, which does the same, and the result is a colony of thousands of genetically identical individuals sharing a single skeleton. Successive generations build on the skeletons of those that came before, and a reef is simply that accumulation, continued for thousands of years. The process is slow. The fastest branching corals add a few centimetres in a year, and the massive, boulder-shaped species far less than that, which is why damage done in an afternoon can take a century to repair.

B The polyp does not work alone. Inside the tissues of every reef-building coral there live enormous numbers of single-celled algae. They are sheltered there, and they are supplied with the waste products of their host, which to a plant are valuable nutrients. In return they photosynthesise, and they pass a large share of the sugars they make to the coral around them. For a coral growing in clear, sunlit water, this internal garden provides most of the energy it needs; the tentacles, which are put out mainly at night to catch drifting plankton, supply the remainder. The algae also account for the appearance of the reef. Coral tissue is itself almost colourless, and the browns, golds and greens of a healthy colony are the colours of the crop it carries. The partnership is what allows corals to grow fast enough to build a structure that the sea is constantly trying to pull down.

C It also explains where reefs can and cannot grow. Because the algae need light, reef-building corals are confined to shallow water, and because they need warmth they are found mostly in a broad belt either side of the equator. They require a firm foundation to settle on, and clear water, since suspended sediment both shuts out the light and settles on the polyps, which must then spend energy cleaning themselves. These conditions are met in the open tropical ocean, and the open tropical ocean is one of the least productive environments on earth, so poor in dissolved nutrients that it is often described as a marine desert. The reef sits in the middle of this desert and supports a quantity of life that is out of all proportion to its surroundings: a small fraction of one per cent of the sea floor shelters something approaching a quarter of all marine species. The explanation lies in the partnership. Nutrients that elsewhere would sink into deep water are passed back and forth within the reef instead of being lost, and very little is wasted.

D That dependence has a cost. When a coral is placed under stress it expels the algae from its tissues, sometimes within a few days. Since the tissue is transparent and the algae are gone, what the eye now sees is the white limestone skeleton beneath, and the colony is said to have bleached. A bleached coral has not died. It has lost the greater part of its food supply, and it is starving. If the stress passes quickly, algae recolonise the tissue over the following weeks or months and the colony regains both its colour and its growth, although it may not reproduce that year. If the stress continues, the coral starves, and the skeleton is soon colonised by seaweeds and worn away by the many organisms that bore into it.

E A number of things can cause bleaching. Very low tides that leave corals exposed to the air will do it, as will sudden floods of fresh water after heavy rain, a heavy load of sediment washed off the land, certain pollutants, and even an unusual cold spell. By far the most important cause, however, is water that is unusually warm for that place and that season. Corals live close to their upper limit for much of the year, and a rise of only a degree or two above the normal summer maximum, held for several weeks, is enough to break the partnership. The distinction matters, because the local causes affect a bay or a stretch of shore, while abnormally warm water can settle over an entire sea for a whole summer and bleach reefs across thousands of kilometres at once.

F When a reef dies, the loss is structural as well as biological. The limestone remains, but nothing is adding to it, and the agents of destruction do not pause: boring sponges and molluscs weaken it from within, grazing fish and urchins scrape at it from without, and storms break up what is left. Within a few years an intricate landscape of branches, overhangs and crevices has been reduced to rubble and a low pavement. The animals that depended on those hiding places go with them, and the fisheries that support many coastal communities decline. Reefs also provide coastal protection, absorbing much of the energy of waves before it reaches the shore, and a flattened reef absorbs far less, so that beaches erode and low-lying land floods more readily than it did. Tourism, which in some countries is a significant part of the national income, suffers in its turn.

G None of this makes the situation hopeless, and a good deal can be done locally. Reducing the sewage, fertiliser and soil that run off the land removes one source of stress and gives a reef a better chance of recovering from another. Protecting the fish that graze on seaweed matters more than it sounds, because after a coral dies the contest for the bare space is between young corals and fast-growing algae, and where grazers have been fished out the algae win. Restoration adds to this: fragments of coral are grown in underwater nurseries on frames suspended above the sea bed, and are later planted on damaged reefs, while researchers are working with colonies that have survived repeated heat and attempting to breed from them. Such work is valuable, but nobody involved in it claims more than that it buys time. A reef can be defended against pollution, against overfishing and against careless anchoring by the people who live beside it; it cannot be defended by them against the temperature of the ocean.

Bước 3 – Đọc One-Shot: đến đoạn nào chốt hết câu hỏi của đoạn đó

Đoạn A – Rạn san hô trông như đá nhưng là đá do động vật tạo ra: polyp lắng đọng đá vôi dưới thân, nhân đôi thành quần thể và các thế hệ chồng lên nhau rất chậm.

  • Câu 1 → v. Câu mở đoạn chính là heading: "rock made by animals" ↔ v "The animals that make the rock". Bẫy: heading vi (san hô biển sâu lạnh) và ii (buôn bán đồ trang trí) không hề có trong bài. Trích: "A coral reef looks like rock, and in a sense it is, but it is rock made by animals."

Đoạn B – Polyp không làm việc một mình: tảo đơn bào sống trong mô san hô quang hợp và chuyển phần lớn đường cho vật chủ, đồng thời tạo nên màu sắc của rạn.

  • Câu 2 → i. Ý chính = quan hệ hợp tác nuôi sống "thợ xây" ↔ i "A partnership that feeds the builders"; builders = polyp ở đoạn A. Bẫy: đoạn cũng nói về màu sắc, nhưng đó chỉ là hệ quả phụ. Trích: "The polyp does not work alone. ... they pass a large share of the sugars they make to the coral around them."
  • Lưu ý: "Gardeners" trong tiêu đề chính là đám tảo này; nắm được điều đó thì các câu sau về bleaching rất dễ.

Đoạn C – Nhu cầu của tảo quy định nơi rạn mọc được (nước nông, ấm, trong, nền cứng), và vì vùng nhiệt đới nghèo dinh dưỡng nên rạn là ốc đảo sự sống giữa "sa mạc biển".

  • Câu 3 → vii. "wealth in the middle of a poor ocean" ↔ sự sống dồi dào giữa "marine desert" nghèo dinh dưỡng → heading vii. Lưu ý đoạn mở bằng điều kiện sống, nhưng trọng tâm nằm ở nghịch lý giàu/nghèo. Trích: "so poor in dissolved nutrients that it is often described as a marine desert. The reef sits in the middle of this desert and supports a quantity of life that is out of all proportion to its surroundings"

Đoạn D – Khi bị căng thẳng, san hô thải tảo ra ngoài và lộ bộ xương đá vôi trắng – gọi là tẩy trắng; san hô chưa chết mà đang đói, có thể hồi phục nếu căng thẳng qua nhanh.

  • Câu 4 → viii. Ý chính = mất màu chưa phải là chết ↔ viii "Why a loss of colour is not yet a death". Trích: "A bleached coral has not died. It has lost the greater part of its food supply, and it is starving."
  • Câu 8 → D. "drives out the ___ that normally live within its tissues" ↔ "expels the algae from its tissues" → D. algae. Bẫy: C. plankton cũng là sinh vật nhỏ nhưng là thức ăn bắt từ bên ngoài, không sống trong mô. Trích: "When a coral is placed under stress it expels the algae from its tissues"
  • Câu 9 → B. "the white ___ underneath becomes visible" ↔ "the white limestone skeleton beneath" → B. skeleton. Trích: "what the eye now sees is the white limestone skeleton beneath"
  • Câu 11 → E. "if ordinary conditions return soon enough it can ___ completely" ↔ "regains both its colour and its growth" → E. recover. Bẫy: G. spread không khớp vì bài nói lấy lại chứ không nói lan rộng. Trích: "If the stress passes quickly, algae recolonise the tissue over the following weeks or months and the colony regains both its colour and its growth"
  • Lưu ý: Bài có lưu ý nhỏ "although it may not reproduce that year": phục hồi không có nghĩa là sinh sản ngay.

Đoạn E – Nhiều nguyên nhân gây tẩy trắng (thủy triều thấp, nước ngọt, trầm tích, ô nhiễm, rét), nhưng quan trọng nhất là nước ấm bất thường, và khác biệt là nguyên nhân địa phương chỉ hại một vịnh còn nước ấm hại cả vùng biển.

  • Câu 5 → iii. Ý chính = điều gì đẩy san hô vượt ngưỡng chịu đựng ↔ iii "What pushes a coral beyond its limits" ("upper limit" xuất hiện nguyên văn). Trích: "Corals live close to their upper limit for much of the year, and a rise of only a degree or two above the normal summer maximum, held for several weeks, is enough to break the partnership."
  • Câu 10 → F. "a spell of unusually high sea ___ lasting for several weeks" ↔ "unusually warm ... held for several weeks" → F. temperature. Bẫy: A. sediment cũng là một nguyên nhân được liệt kê, nhưng câu hỏi hỏi nguyên nhân phổ biến NHẤT và cần một danh từ đi với "high". Trích: "By far the most important cause, however, is water that is unusually warm for that place and that season."

Đoạn F – Khi rạn chết, cấu trúc cũng mất: đá vôi bị khoan, gặm, bão đánh vỡ thành đống vụn, kéo theo mất nơi trú ẩn, mất nghề cá, mất khả năng chắn sóng và mất du lịch.

  • Câu 6 → x. Ý chính = những gì xảy ra sau khi cấu trúc rạn biến mất ↔ x "What follows once the structure goes"; đoạn mở đầu cũng nói "the loss is structural as well as biological". Đoạn G – Tình hình chưa tuyệt vọng: giảm nước thải và đất trôi, bảo vệ cá ăn tảo, nuôi mảnh san hô trong vườn ươm dưới nước rồi trồng lại – nhưng tất cả chỉ "mua thêm thời gian" vì không ai chống được nhiệt độ đại dương.

  • Câu 7 → iv. Ý chính = các biện pháp có ích nhưng có giới hạn ↔ iv "Measures that help, and the limit of their help". Bẫy: heading ix (thuốc mới từ rạn) không có trong bài. Trích: "Such work is valuable, but nobody involved in it claims more than that it buys time."

Bẫy cần tránh

  • Trong danh sách headings có ba lựa chọn không hề xuất hiện trong bài (ii, vi, ix). Loại chúng sớm để chọn nhanh hơn.
  • Nhiều đoạn có hai lớp ý (điều kiện sống + nghịch lý giàu nghèo ở đoạn C; hậu quả + du lịch ở đoạn F): heading luôn khớp với ý bao trùm, không phải chi tiết cuối đoạn.
  • Với summary dùng hộp từ, hãy xét từ loại và cụm đi kèm: "high sea ___" bắt buộc là một danh từ đo được (temperature), "it can ___ completely" bắt buộc là động từ.
